Research profile

Reliable AI systems require evidence beyond successful outputs.

My research is guided by a broad question: how can intelligent systems be evaluated, governed, observed, and made reliable in complex real-world environments?

This question connects my work across agentic AI, multimodal systems, and applied NLP, from clinical documentation and medical monitoring to workflow orchestration and model evaluation.

I use system architecture and experimental evaluation to examine behavior during operation: decisions, tool use, temporal events, and failure modes. Observability and reproducible experiments provide evidence for assessing reliability and limitations.

Current research collaboration

Intelligent monitoring methods for medical and technological processes in the operating room based on video and speech analytics

ITMO University research collaboration · Ongoing

I am currently contributing to research on intelligent monitoring of medical and technological processes in the operating room using video and speech analytics. The work explores event-based multimodal analysis: combining video, speech and acoustic cues to identify temporal episodes, deviations from clinical protocols, and clinically meaningful events.

My contribution is centered on the AI/ML side of the collaboration — video/audio pipelines, event extraction, model comparison, experimental evaluation, analysis, and manuscript preparation.
