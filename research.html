Research that moves from systems to evidence.

I study how intelligent systems can be evaluated, governed, observed, and made reliable in complex real-world environments. My research spans agentic systems, multimodal AI, model evaluation and applied NLP.

Current collaboration · ITMO University

Intelligent monitoring methods for medical and technological processes in the operating room based on video and speech analytics

ITMO University research collaboration · Ongoing

This ongoing project investigates how video, audio and speech signals can support monitoring of operating-room activity as a sequence of temporal events. The aim is to identify clinically meaningful episodes and protocol deviations over time.

The methodology under development uses clinical expertise to define observable events and guide temporal annotation. Experimental evaluation is being designed to compare video, audio and multimodal pipelines against these expert-grounded definitions as part of the ongoing research.

My research contribution

I contribute on the AI/ML side: video and audio pipelines, temporal event extraction, model comparison, experimental design and evaluation, analysis, and manuscript preparation. These activities are ongoing.

Core themes

What connects my research

Multi-Agent Systems

I study how LLM-based agents coordinate, divide work, make decisions and interact with governed execution environments.

Agent Evaluation & Observability

I study agent behavior through execution traces, decisions, tool use and failure modes, with an emphasis on reproducible experiments and interpretable evaluation.

Trustworthy AI

I investigate safeguards, approval boundaries and human oversight, and how their effects on system behavior can be evaluated experimentally.

Multimodal & Applied NLP

I am expanding from text-centric agent systems toward multimodal research where speech, video and structured domain knowledge must work together.

MSc thesis · Ongoing

Centralized vs. decentralized LLM-based multi-agent architectures

My MSc thesis at Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University examines the advantages and disadvantages of centralized and decentralized multi-agent system architectures based on large language models.

The broader goal is to understand how architectural choices affect coordination, control, execution behavior, reliability and evaluation. The comparison is ongoing and connects to broader questions about the design and assessment of intelligent systems.

Recent research thread

Browser-grounded multimodal agents

Manuscript submission

At SMILES 2026, I worked on WebPilot, which asks a practical agentic-AI question: what changes when a coding agent can inspect the browser, observe what actually happened, and repair its own output instead of stopping after code generation?

The framework combines visual screenshots, DOM state, console and build logs, and interaction feedback. This work connects directly with my broader interest in observability and evaluation: intelligent systems should be judged through evidence from their behavior, not only the text or code they produce.
