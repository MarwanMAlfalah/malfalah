Research that moves from systems to evidence.

I am interested in intelligent systems that must coordinate, explain themselves, and remain dependable in environments where mistakes matter. My work combines multi-agent architectures, observability, evaluation, multimodal AI and applied NLP.

Current collaboration · ITMO University

Intelligent monitoring methods for medical and technological processes in the operating room based on video and speech analytics

Ongoing research collaboration targeting a Q1 journal submission

The project studies operating-room activity as a sequence of temporal events rather than isolated frames. The research direction combines video with audio and speech signals to identify clinically relevant episodes, protocol deviations and errors over time.

A central part of the methodology is expert-grounded evaluation. Clinical knowledge is translated into observable event definitions and temporal annotation, while multimodal pipelines are compared experimentally rather than assumed to be better.

My research contribution

I contribute on the AI/ML side: video and audio pipelines, temporal event extraction, model comparison, experimental design, analysis, and preparation of the international manuscript.

Core themes

What connects my research

Multi-Agent Systems

I study how LLM-based agents coordinate, divide work, make decisions and interact with governed execution environments.

Agent Evaluation & Observability

I care about what can be measured after an agent acts: traces, decisions, tool use, failure modes, reproducibility and interpretable evaluation.

Trustworthy AI

My systems increasingly emphasize safeguards, approval boundaries, human oversight and measurable behavior instead of opaque success claims.

Multimodal & Applied NLP

I am expanding from text-centric agent systems toward multimodal research where speech, video and structured domain knowledge must work together.

MSc thesis

Centralized vs. decentralized LLM-based multi-agent architectures

My MSc thesis at Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University examines the advantages and disadvantages of centralized and decentralized multi-agent system architectures based on large language models.

The broader goal is to understand how architectural choices affect coordination, control, reliability and evaluation — questions that also shape my work on industrial orchestration and agent observability.

Recent research thread

Browser-grounded multimodal agents

At SMILES 2026, I worked on WebPilot, which asks a practical agentic-AI question: what changes when a coding agent can inspect the browser, observe what actually happened, and repair its own output instead of stopping after code generation?

The framework combines visual screenshots, DOM state, console and build logs, and interaction feedback. This work connects directly with my broader interest in observability and evaluation: intelligent systems should be judged through evidence from their behavior, not only the text or code they produce.
